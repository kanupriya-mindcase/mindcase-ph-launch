Decisions locked

Launch from a founder account

@kritish, not a brand account, and no paid hunter. The maker's own network drives the result now.

Goal is signups

Everything points visitors to sign up. A PH-exclusive "30% off, first 100" offer reduces friction.

Warm-up is the critical path

Account age can't be rushed. 5 genuine comments/day, pure value, never pitching Mindcase.

Track everything

UTM link (utm_source=producthunt) so GA4 shows exactly what the launch drove.

How to comment (warm-up)

The point is to look like a real, engaged peer — never a bot, and never someone who didn't read the page. Each product gets 4 draft options across three modes: a genuine validation ("this was needed — congrats on the launch"), a real non-obvious question a practitioner would actually wonder, and a knowledgeable take with a bit of intrigue — one that names the interesting tension in the space, not just praise. Aim for 2–3 lines each: in-depth through substance, not padding.

Do

· Read the launch page first — only ask what genuinely isn't answered there.
· Validate when it's true — specific and grounded, not empty flattery.
· Ask genuine, non-obvious questions (architecture, edge cases, tradeoffs).
· Land one knowledgeable-with-intrigue take — name the real hard part, then get curious how they solved it.
· 2–3 lines each. Depth via insight, never filler.

Don't

· Ask something the page already answers. It signals you didn't read it and makes Kritish look like a fool.
· Faff or filler. No "congrats 🎉 looks awesome."
· Use em-dashes (—). They read AI-generated. Use a period, comma, colon, or rewrite the sentence instead.
· Describe how it solves a use-case for us. This isn't about Mindcase, and it reads as a soft pitch.
· Pitch Mindcase. Ever.

If the interesting question turns out to be answered on the launch page, switch to a validation option instead. Better to say "this was needed, nice launch" than to ask something that's sitting right there.
